# White Fig Clafoutis

**Makes** 6 servings · **Total** 1 hr 15 · **Active** 15 min

Classic all-milk clafoutis — a soft, custardy baked batter over white figs. Barely sweet by design, so the figs lead. No cream needed.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| White figs | 5–6 large or 8 medium (350g) |
| Eggs, large | 5 (280g) |
| Plain flour | 120 |
| Caster sugar (batter) | 80 |
| Whole milk | 400ml (410g) |
| Vanilla extract | 1 tsp (5g) |
| Salt | ¼ tsp (1.5g) |
| Butter, for greasing | 20 |
| Demerara sugar, for dusting the dish | 20 |
| Icing sugar, to serve | 5 |

## Method

1. **Prep the dish** · 5 min

   Heat the oven to 180°C (160°C fan / gas 4). Grease a 25cm baking or gratin dish generously with butter (**20g**), then dust the base and sides with demerara sugar (**20g**), tipping out the excess. The sugar gives crisp, caramelised edges and helps release.

2. **Arrange the figs** · 3 min

   Cut the 5–6 large white figs (**350g**) into sixths or ~1cm wedges (skin on), blot the cut sides on kitchen paper, and lay them skin-side down in a single layer covering the base. Don't crowd the centre — too many wet figs there stops it setting.

3. **Whisk the base** · 2 min

   Whisk the 5 large eggs (**280g**) with the caster sugar (**80g**) for 1–2 min until pale and slightly thickened.

4. **Make the batter** · 3 min

   Sift in the plain flour (**120g**) and ¼ tsp (**1.5g**) salt, whisk to a smooth thick paste, then gradually whisk in 400ml (**410g**) whole milk and 1 tsp (**5g**) vanilla until smooth and thin — about single-cream consistency. Don't over-whisk once the milk is in, or it souffles up then collapses. Sieve if any lumps remain.

5. **Rest the batter** · 10 min

   Leave the batter to rest so the flour hydrates and sets better. Skim off any foam before pouring.

6. **Bake** · 35–40 min

   Pour the batter gently around the figs to ~2.5–3cm deep. Bake for 35–40 min until puffed, golden, and just set with a slight wobble in the centre. Test *between* the figs, not through one (a fig gives a false wet reading). Tent loosely with foil if the top browns before the middle sets.

7. **Cool and serve** · 15 min

   Rest for 15 min — it will sink a little, which is normal. Dust with icing sugar (**5g**) and serve warm.

## Tips

- Skin-side down (cut faces up): the exposed flesh caramelises and moisture escapes upward instead of pooling under the fruit.
- Big or soft figs: cut smaller (sixths / ~1cm) and blot the cut sides — cuts down water bleeding into the batter and a soggy middle.
- Depth is the key variable: keep to ~2.5–3cm. Too deep and the top sets before the centre — the main way clafoutis fails. For a smaller dish, scale the batter down rather than filling deep (roughly ⅔ of this batter suits a small oval covered by ~3 figs).
- Caster in the batter, demerara on the dish. Brown sugar in the batter muddies the colour and adds unwanted moisture.
- All-milk is the traditional method — whole milk gives the best texture; semi-skimmed works but is slightly less rich.
- Wet-fig insurance (Julia Child trick): pour a thin layer of batter, bake ~5 min to just set, then add the figs and the rest of the batter so they don't sink.
- If it's still sloshing after ~50 min, drop to 170°C, tent with foil, and move to a lower shelf — a wet batter just needs longer and gentler heat for the egg to set.
