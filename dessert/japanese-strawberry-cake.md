# Japanese Strawberry Cake

**Makes** 1 cake (20cm round, 8 servings) · **Total** 3 hr 30 · **Active** ~1 hr

Japanese bakery-style strawberry shortcake: a light, fluffy sponge baked in a water bath, layered with lightly sweet whipped cream and macerated strawberries. Gelatin in the cream is optional; it keeps the cake's shape for longer.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Cake flour | 75 |
| Egg yolks, large | 4 (~70g) |
| Egg whites, large | 4 (~120g) |
| Caster sugar, for the meringue | 70 |
| Strawberries, to slice | 225 |
| Caster sugar, for the strawberries | 1½ tsp (6g) |
| Strawberries, whole, for the top | 115 |
| Whipping cream (heavy cream) | 280 |
| Icing sugar | 2 tbsp (16g) |
| Gelatin powder (optional, stabilised cream) | ¾ tsp (2.5g) |
| Cold water, for the gelatin (optional) | 2 tbsp (30g) |
| Caster sugar, for the syrup | 2 tbsp (25g) |
| Hot water, for the syrup | 3 tbsp (45g) |

### Milk and butter bowl (step 2)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Whole milk | 80 |
| Unsalted butter | 50 |

## Method

1. **Prep everything** · 15 min

   Line the base of a 20cm round tin with baking paper. If it's a springform or loose-bottomed tin, wrap the outside in a few layers of foil so the water bath can't leak in. Find a deep roasting tin that the cake tin fits inside. Fit the whisk to the Kenwood and make sure the bowl and whisk are clean and dry; any fat stops the whites whipping. Weigh the cake flour (**75g**) out for step 3. Separate the eggs: 4 yolks (**~70g**) into a small bowl for step 3, 4 whites (**~120g**) straight into the Kenwood bowl for step 4. Weigh the caster sugar (**70g**) into a small bowl by the mixer.

2. **Heat the oven, melt the butter** · 5 min

   Heat the oven to 160°C with a rack in the middle and boil a kettle for the water bath. Microwave the milk and butter bowl (heatproof) until the butter has melted, then stir.

3. **Make the yolk batter** · 5 min

   Sift the cake flour (**75g**) into the milk and butter and stir gently with a spatula until smooth. Stir in the 4 yolks (**~70g**) until evenly combined. Mix this by hand so the Kenwood bowl stays free for the whites.

4. **Whip the whites** · 8 min

   Whisk the 4 whites (**~120g**) in the Kenwood on medium-high speed until frothy. With the mixer still running, add the caster sugar (**70g**) a little at a time. Keep whisking until the meringue is glossy and holds medium peaks.

5. **Fold** · 5 min

   Fold a quarter of the whites into the yolk batter with a spatula until smooth. Scrape that back into the whites in the Kenwood bowl and fold by hand until just smooth, with no big streaks of white. Don't use the machine for this; it knocks the air out.

6. **Fill the tin, set the water bath** · 5 min

   Pour the batter into the lined tin. Drop the tin onto the worktop twice from about 12cm up to release large bubbles. Stand it in the roasting tin and pour in hot water from the kettle to 2.5cm up the side of the cake tin.

7. **Bake** · 1 hr 30

   Bake at 160°C for 1 hr 30, without opening the oven door for the first hour. It's done when a skewer in the centre comes out clean and the cake has pulled away from the sides of the tin.

8. **Cool the cake** · ~1 hr

   Run a knife around the edge to release the cake and turn it out onto a cooling rack. Cool completely before cutting or decorating.

9. **Macerate the strawberries** · 1–2 hr (while the cake cools)

   Slice the strawberries (**225g**) into 5mm slices and put them in a bowl with 1½ tsp (**6g**) caster sugar. Toss to coat and leave for at least 1 hour and up to 2, until glossy and sweet. Lift the slices out onto a plate and keep the liquid left in the bowl. Keep the whole strawberries (**115g**) aside for the top.

10. **Make the cake syrup** · 2 min

    Stir 2 tbsp (**25g**) caster sugar into 3 tbsp (**45g**) hot water until dissolved. Optionally stir in the reserved strawberry liquid for extra flavour; it tints the cake slices slightly pink.

11. **Whip the cream** · 10 min

    Choose plain or stabilised cream. Stabilised cream sets, so make it only once the cake is ready to assemble.

    - **Plain:** wash and dry the Kenwood bowl and whisk. Pour in the cream (**280g**), sift in 2 tbsp (**16g**) icing sugar and whisk on medium speed until firm peaks form.
    - **Stabilised:** stir ¾ tsp (**2.5g**) gelatin into 2 tbsp (**30g**) cold water, leave for 5 min, then melt briefly in the microwave or a hot water bath. Wash and dry the bowl and whisk, pour in the cream (**280g**), sift in 2 tbsp (**16g**) icing sugar and whisk on medium speed to very soft peaks, then stop. Stir a large scoop of cream into the melted gelatin, drizzle that back into the bowl and whisk briefly until soft peaks form. Don't over-whip or the gelatin separates into blobs.

12. **Level and split the cake** · 10 min

    Put the cooled cake on a board and measure its height at 4 points. Mark the cut for the top and the cut for halving with toothpicks. Slice off the browned top along the toothpicks with a serrated knife and discard it, then slice the cake into two even layers. Put the top layer cut side down on a turntable (or an upturned bowl with a plate on top) and brush the syrup (**70g** in total) evenly over it. Flip the other layer upside down and brush syrup over that too.

13. **Fill the cake** · 10 min

    Spoon a large dollop of the whipped cream (**280g** in total) onto the layer on the turntable. Spread it with an offset spatula into a thin, even layer, until it just spills over the edge. Cover with as many macerated strawberry slices (**225g**) as fit. Add another large dollop of cream over the strawberries and spread it thinly. Set the second layer on top, lined up with the bottom one.

14. **Cover and decorate** · 15 min

    Spread another dollop of cream over the top. Use the cream that has spilled over to smooth the sides, adding more if needed, until the cake has a thin, even coat. Add a larger dollop to the top, spread it thicker and smooth the sides again. Pipe any leftover cream onto the top or sides with a piping bag, or a freezer bag with the corner cut off. Decorate with the whole strawberries (**115g**) on top.

15. **Chill and serve** · 30 min+

    - **Stabilised cream:** refrigerate uncovered for at least 30 min to set. For the best texture, let it come back to room temperature for about 1 hour before slicing.
    - **Plain cream:** serve straight away, or refrigerate and serve within a few hours. Slice straight from the fridge.

    Slice either with a serrated knife.

## Tips

- **Kenwood use**: only the whites (step 4) and the cream (step 11) use the mixer, with the balloon whisk. The yolk batter and all folding stay by hand, since the machine would knock the air out. Wash and dry the bowl and whisk between the two. A stand mixer takes cream from soft peaks to over-whipped quickly, so watch it and stop early.
- **Vegetarian**: skip the gelatin and make the plain cream (from the source).
- **No turntable**: stand an upturned bowl or cake tin on the worktop and put a flat plate, or the removable base of a pie tin, on top (from the source).
- **Strawberry amount**: the source gives 340–450g in total. This uses the low end: **225g** sliced plus **115g** for the top. Add up to **225g** on top for a fuller finish.
