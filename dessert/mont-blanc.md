# Mont Blanc

**Makes** one 22cm cake, 8 slices · **Total** 3 hr 45 · **Active** 1 hr 45

Japanese café-style chestnut cake made from fresh chestnuts: a split sponge filled and domed with whipped cream, then covered in piped strands of homemade chestnut cream.

## Method

1. **Whisk the sponge** · 10 min

   Heat the oven to 180°C (160°C fan). Line the 26cm square cake tin with baking paper.

   Fit the mixer bowl to the Kenwood with the balloon whisk. Whisk on high for 8–10 min, until thick and pale and a trail of batter sits on the surface for a few seconds.

2. **Fold and bake** · 25 min

   Warm the butter and milk jug in the microwave until the butter melts.

   Sift the plain flour (**150g**) over the eggs in 3 lots, folding each in gently by hand with a spatula. Stir a large scoop of batter into the warm butter and milk, then fold that back in.

   Pour into the tin and bake for 20–25 min, until golden and the top springs back when pressed. Start step 3 while it bakes.

   When it's done, leave it in the tin for 5 min, then turn it out onto a rack to cool completely.

3. **Score, boil and peel the chestnuts** · 50 min

   Pick 3 of the best-looking chestnuts for the top. Put each one flat side down on a board and cut one slit across the rounded side, through the shell and just into the flesh.

   Cut the rest of the chestnuts (**~600g** in all) in half across their width with a sharp serrated knife. Hold each one with a folded tea towel and keep your fingers clear, since they slip. Soaking them in hot water for 30 min first softens the shell and makes cutting easier.

   Put them all in a large pan, cover with cold water and bring to the boil. Simmer for 20 min, then lift out the halves. Simmer the 3 whole ones for 5–10 min more, until a knife goes in easily.

   Scoop the flesh out of the halves with a teaspoon; the inner skin stays in the shell. You need **400g**. Peel the 3 whole chestnuts while they're hot, taking off the shell and the brown inner skin.

4. **Make the chestnut cream** · 10 min

   Add the peeled chestnuts (**400g**) to the chestnut pan. Simmer over low heat for 5–10 min, stirring, until the chestnuts are soft and most of the milk is absorbed.

5. **Blend, sieve and chill** · 10 min

   Blend the hot chestnut mix with the soft unsalted butter (**40g**) until smooth.

   Push it through a fine sieve with the back of a spoon. This is slow, but it's what makes the strands smooth enough to pipe.

   Spread it in a shallow dish so it cools fast, cover and chill while you do steps 6–8, at least 30 min.

6. **Cut the sponge** · 5 min

   Put a 22cm plate on the cooled sponge and cut round it. Slice the round in half horizontally into 2 layers.

7. **Whip the cream** · 5 min

   Wash and dry the Kenwood bowl and whisk. Whisk the cold double cream (**~410g**) and caster sugar (**30g**) on medium speed until it holds firm peaks. Watch it, since the Kenwood over-whips cream quickly.

8. **Fill and dome** · 30 min

   Put the bottom layer on a plate and spread over a third of the cream. Add the top layer.

   Pile the rest of the cream on top in a low dome, leaving a 1cm edge of sponge. Chill for 20 min so the cream firms up.

9. **Pipe the chestnut cream** · 15 min

   Beat the chilled chestnut cream with double cream (**40–60g**), a little at a time, until it pipes in a smooth line without breaking but still holds its shape.

   With a Mont Blanc nozzle, pipe strands in circles from the edge up to the centre, covering the cream dome.

   Put the 3 whole chestnuts on top and dust with icing sugar (1 tsp, **~5g**).

10. **Chill** · 1 hr

    Chill for 1 hr before slicing, so the cream sets. Keeps 2 days in the fridge.

## Tips

- **Ready-cooked chestnuts:** with vacuum-packed peeled chestnuts, skip step 3. Use **400g**, plus 3 whole ones for the top.
- **Make ahead:** the chestnut cream (to the end of step 5) keeps 3 days in the fridge. The sponge, wrapped in cling film once cool, keeps 1 day. With both made the day before, the day itself takes about 1 hr plus the final chill.
- **No Mont Blanc nozzle:** push the chestnut cream through a potato ricer or garlic press and drape the strands over the dome.
- **Square cake:** skip cutting the round and make a 26cm square cake instead. You may need a little more cream.
- **Chestnut cream consistency:** if it breaks as you pipe, beat in a little more double cream. If it's too soft to hold strands, chill it for 15 min.
- **Kenwood use:** the balloon whisk does the eggs and the cream. The flour and butter are folded in by hand, since the machine would knock the air out.
- **Sponge offcuts:** the corners left after cutting the round are good for trifle or a snack.
