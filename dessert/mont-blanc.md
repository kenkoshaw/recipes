# Mont Blanc

**Makes** one 18cm cake, 6 slices · **Total** 3 hr 50 · **Active** 1 hr 45

Japanese café-style chestnut cake made from fresh chestnuts: two sponge layers filled and domed with whipped cream, then covered in piped strands of homemade chestnut cream.

## Method

1. **Warm and whisk the eggs** · 15 min

   Heat the oven to 180°C (160°C fan). Line the bases of both 18cm round sponge tins with baking paper.

   Set the mixer bowl over a pan of barely simmering water, without touching the water. Whisk by hand for 2–3 min, until the sugar dissolves and the eggs feel warm, about 40°C. Warm eggs whip up much higher than cold ones.

   Fit the bowl to the Kenwood with the balloon whisk. Whisk on high for 10–12 min, until the mix is nearly white and about 3 times its starting volume. Lift the whisk: the ribbon of batter should sit on the surface for 5 seconds before sinking.

   If it's still yellow, keep whisking. Whole eggs never reach stiff peaks, so don't wait for them. Finish with 1 min on low to even out the bubbles.

2. **Fold and bake** · 25 min

   Warm the butter and milk jug in the microwave until the butter melts.

   Sift the plain flour (**120g**) over the eggs in 3 lots. Fold each in by hand with a spatula: cut down through the middle, sweep along the bottom and up the side, turning the bowl as you go. Stop as soon as no flour shows.

   Stir a large scoop of batter into the warm butter and milk, then fold that back in the same way. Get it into the tins and oven straight away, before the bubbles fade.

   Divide the batter evenly between the 2 tins, about **250g** each. Bake for 15–20 min, until golden and the tops spring back when pressed. Start step 3 while they bake.

   When they're done, leave them in the tins for 5 min, then turn them out onto a rack, peel off the paper and cool completely.

3. **Score, boil and peel the chestnuts** · 50 min

   Pick 3 of the best-looking chestnuts for the top. Put each one flat side down on a board and cut one slit across the rounded side, through the shell and just into the flesh.

   Cut the rest of the chestnuts (**~450g** in all) in half across their width with a sharp serrated knife. Hold each one with a folded tea towel and keep your fingers clear, since they slip. Soaking them in hot water for 30 min first softens the shell and makes cutting easier.

   Put them all in a large pan, cover with cold water and bring to the boil. Simmer for 20 min, then lift out the halves. Simmer the 3 whole ones for 5–10 min more, until a knife goes in easily.

   Scoop the flesh out of the halves with a teaspoon; the inner skin stays in the shell. You need **300g**. Peel the 3 whole chestnuts while they're hot, taking off the shell and the brown inner skin.

4. **Make the chestnut cream** · 10 min

   Add the peeled chestnuts (**300g**) to the chestnut pan. Simmer over low heat for 5–10 min, stirring, until the chestnuts are soft and most of the milk is absorbed.

5. **Blend, sieve and chill** · 10 min

   Blend the hot chestnut mix with the soft unsalted butter (**30g**) until smooth.

   Push it through a fine sieve with the back of a spoon. This is slow, but it's what makes the strands smooth enough to pipe.

   Spread it in a shallow dish so it cools fast, cover and chill while you do steps 6–8, at least 30 min.

6. **Level the sponges** · 5 min

   If either sponge has domed, trim the top flat with a serrated knife.

7. **Whip the cream** · 5 min

   Wash and dry the Kenwood bowl and whisk. Whisk the cold double cream (**~310g**) and caster sugar (**20g**) on medium speed until it holds firm peaks. Watch it, since the Kenwood over-whips cream quickly.

8. **Fill and dome** · 30 min

   Put one sponge on a plate and spread over a third of the cream. Put the second sponge on top, upside down, so its flat base is on top.

   Pile the rest of the cream on top in a low dome, leaving a 1cm edge of sponge. Chill for 20 min so the cream firms up.

9. **Pipe the chestnut cream** · 15 min

   Beat the chilled chestnut cream with double cream (**30–45g**), a little at a time, until it pipes in a smooth line without breaking but still holds its shape.

   With a Mont Blanc nozzle, pipe strands in circles from the edge up to the centre, covering the cream dome.

   Put the 3 whole chestnuts on top and dust with icing sugar (1 tsp, **~5g**).

10. **Chill** · 1 hr

    Chill for 1 hr before slicing, so the cream sets. Keeps 2 days in the fridge.

## Tips

- **Ready-cooked chestnuts:** with vacuum-packed peeled chestnuts, skip step 3. Use **300g**, plus 3 whole ones for the top.
- **Make ahead:** the chestnut cream (to the end of step 5) keeps 3 days in the fridge. The sponges, wrapped in cling film once cool, keep 1 day. With both made the day before, the day itself takes about 1 hr plus the final chill.
- **No Mont Blanc nozzle:** push the chestnut cream through a potato ricer or garlic press and drape the strands over the dome.
- **Chestnut cream consistency:** if it breaks as you pipe, beat in a little more double cream. If it's too soft to hold strands, chill it for 15 min.
- **Dense sponge:** almost always under-whipped eggs. The mix must turn nearly white before the flour goes in; yellow and thick isn't enough. Cold eggs and overfolding also knock it down.
- **Kenwood use:** the balloon whisk does the eggs and the cream. The flour and butter are folded in by hand, since the machine would knock the air out.
