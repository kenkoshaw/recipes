# Zwetschgendatschi

**Makes** 9 squares · **Total** 2 hr 30 · **Active** 45 min

Bavarian plum sheet cake: a thin yeasted base packed with prune plums (Zwetschgen) pressed in cut side up, under a cinnamon streusel. Sized to the 26×26cm square cake tin and 1.1kg of plums. Kenwood-kneaded.

## Method

1. **Knead the dough** · 15 min

   Fit the dough hook to the mixer bowl. Run on minimum for 1 min, then speed 1 for 4 min.

   Add the soft butter (**45g**) a piece at a time, then knead on speed 1 for 5 min more. The dough should be smooth, soft and only slightly tacky.

   If the hook only spins the small dough round, tip it out and finish with 5 min of kneading by hand.

2. **First rise** · 50 min

   Shape the dough into a ball and put it in a lightly greased separate bowl, so the Kenwood bowl is free for the streusel. Cover it.

   Put it in the oven with only the light on (about 25–30°C) until doubled, about 50 min. Go by the doubling, not the clock.

3. **Make the streusel** · 2 min

   Wash and dry the Kenwood bowl and fit the K-beater. Mix the streusel bowl on speed 1 for 1–2 min until it forms clumps.

   Put it in the fridge until step 6.

4. **Roll out the base** · 10 min

   Grease and line the 26×26cm square tin. Roll the dough out thin to a square of about 28cm, lay it in the tin and push it slightly up the sides.

   Scatter the semolina (**~15g**) over the base.

5. **Top with the plums and rise** · 45 min

   Lay the plum halves (**1100g**) cut side up in tight rows, each row leaning on the last like roof tiles, and press them lightly into the dough. Sprinkle with 1 tbsp (**12g**) caster sugar.

   Cover loosely and rise for 30 min. Give it the first 15 min in the oven with the light on, then lift the tin out to a warm spot for the last 15 min while the oven heats.

6. **Heat the oven and add the streusel** · 15 min

   Heat the oven to 200°C conventional or 180°C fan, with a shelf in the lower-middle position.

   Scatter the chilled streusel evenly over the plums just before baking.

7. **Bake** · 35–45 min

   Bake on the lower-middle shelf for 35–45 min.

   It's done when the streusel is golden, the plums are soft but hold their shape, and the dough edge and underside are lightly browned. Lift a corner of the paper to check the underside.

8. **Cool** · 30 min

   Leave in the tin for at least 30 min so the juices set. Cut into 9 squares and serve lukewarm.

9. **Whip the cream** · 3 min

   Wash and dry the Kenwood bowl and fit the balloon whisk. Whisk the cold double cream 300ml (**300g**) with 1 tbsp (**~8g**) icing sugar on speed 4 until soft peaks form. Serve with the squares.

## Tips

- **Plum moth**: some plums had small holes. Check each half when stoning and bin any with tunnels or frass inside.
- **Dry fruit, crumb layer**: well-dried, firm plums and the semolina layer stop the base going soggy (Lecker; Swabian recipes use breadcrumbs).
- **Cut side up, roof tiles**: rows leaning on each other and pressed in is the traditional look (Lecker, Augsburg). "Datschen" means to press in.
- **Cool kitchen**: at about 14°C the dough barely rises on the worktop, so both rises use the oven with only the light on.
- **Low shelf**: the base should brown lightly underneath (Der Standard, Austrian Zwetschkenfleck), so bake on the lower-middle shelf.
- **Augsburg finish (no streusel)**: leave out the streusel, dot the plums with butter flakes halfway through baking, then dust with cinnamon sugar straight out of the oven.
- **Storage**: 1–2 days covered at room temperature, up to 3 days in the fridge (the base firms up). Freezes in squares for up to 3 months.
- **Sources checked**: Lecker (Oma's original), the Bayerisch-Schwaben tourist board heritage recipe, Neff, Thermomix Cookidoo, Infranken, Backdorf, Bake to the Roots and Der Standard. The plum weight per cm² here matches Lecker's (about 1.6g per cm²), and hydration is about 60%, also as Lecker.
