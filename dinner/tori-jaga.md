# Tori-jaga

**Makes** 2 servings · **Total** 45 min · **Active** 15 min

鶏じゃが — soy-braised chicken thigh and potato, the chicken counterpart to nikujaga. Built without mirin or sake: dry white wine covers both, with extra sugar to replace mirin's sweetness. Serve teishoku style with a separate bowl of white rice (not included below).

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Boneless chicken thighs, 4cm pieces | 400 |
| Vegetable oil | 14 |
| Dry white wine | 5 tbsp (75g) |
| Spring onions, sliced | 20 |

### Onion and ginger bowl (step 2)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Onion, cut into wedges | 150 |
| Fresh ginger, grated | 10 |

### Potato and carrot bowl (step 3)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Maris Piper potatoes, peeled, 4cm chunks | 500 |
| Carrot, roll-cut | 100 |

### Braising liquid jug (step 5)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Water | 300 |
| Katsuo dashi granules | 1 level tsp (2g) |
| Soy sauce | 2½ tbsp (45g) |
| Caster sugar | 1 tbsp (20g) |

## Method

1. **Brown the chicken** · 4 min

   Heat the oil (**14g**) in a wide heavy pan over medium-high until it shimmers. Lay the chicken (**400g**) in a single layer, fat side down, and leave it undisturbed. Colour only — it finishes in the braise, so don't chase cooked-through.

2. **Onion and ginger** · 2 min

   Tip in the onion and ginger bowl. Stir until the onion edges have gone translucent and the ginger is fragrant.

3. **Potatoes and carrot in** · 1 min

   Drain the potato and carrot bowl and tip it in. Toss to coat everything in the fat.

4. **Boil off the wine** · 2 min

   Pour in the 5 tbsp (**75g**) of white wine and raise to high. Let it bubble hard, scraping the fond off the base. Don't skip this — mirin and sake are low-acid, wine isn't, and raw alcohol left in makes the braise read as a French stew.

5. **Stock, soy and sugar** · 2 min

   Stir the braising liquid jug and pour it in. Bring to a boil, skim the grey scum off the surface, then drop to a bare simmer.

6. **Simmer covered** · 20 min

   Lay a cartouche of baking paper straight onto the surface, or cover loosely with a lid. Stir gently once halfway. Done when a knife slides into a potato chunk with no resistance.

7. **Reduce to a glaze** · 5 min

   Uncover, raise the heat slightly and reduce until the sauce is glossy and coats the potatoes rather than pooling. Tilt the pan and spoon it over a few times. If the potatoes aren't tender yet, add a splash of water and keep going.

8. **Season and finish** · 1 min

   Taste. More soy if it needs salt, a pinch more sugar if it's sharp. Scatter the spring onions (**20g**) on at the last second, off the heat.

## Tips

- **No mirin or sake**: 5 tbsp dry white wine (**75g**) replaces both the mirin and the sake, with caster sugar raised to cover the lost sweetness. Use a dry, unoaked white — Sauvignon Blanc or Pinot Grigio. Oaked Chardonnay fights the soy.
- **Soy reduced for salted dashi**: the Don Quijote 情熱価格 katsuo granules are pre-salted, so soy drops from 3 tbsp to 2½ (**45g**). Taste at step 8 before adding more.
- **Dashi dose**: Marutomo's guidance is a heaped tsp (4g) per 600ml, so 300ml takes a level tsp (**2g**).
- **Potato choice**: Maris Piper partly collapse and thicken the sauce, which is wanted here. Charlotte or other waxy potatoes hold cleaner chunks if you prefer them distinct.
- **Sugar is structural**: it's what gives the glaze body at step 7, not just sweetness. Cutting it too far leaves thin soy-water instead of a coating sauce.
- **Serving**: teishoku style — rice in its own bowl, braise in a separate shallow bowl. As a donburi the potatoes mash into the rice. Warm the bowls; the sauce sets as it cools.
- **Rice timing**: start the rice at step 5. Twelve minutes cooking plus a ten-minute rest lands with the covered simmer and reduction.
- **Cuts through it well**: quick cucumber sunomono — sliced thin, rice vinegar, pinch of sugar and salt, 10 minutes.
- **Leftovers**: better the next day, keeps 3 days. Reheat gently with a splash of water.
- **Potato and carrot bowl**: fill it with cold water so the potatoes don't discolour; drain it when it goes in.
