# Sicilian Prawn and Tomato Spaghetti

**Makes** 2 servings · **Total** 25 min · **Active** 25 min

Anchovy-and-chilli base, cherry tomatoes, no cheese. Built for peeled frozen prawns, so tomato purée stands in for the prawn-shell stock. Anchovy toast on the side replaces the traditional toasted breadcrumbs.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Spaghetti (bronze-die) | 200 |
| Raw king prawns, peeled, thawed and patted bone dry | 300 |
| Cherry tomatoes, halved | 250 |
| Tomato purée | 15 |
| Dry white wine | 100 |
| Olive oil, for the pan | 55 |
| Extra virgin olive oil, to finish | 15 |
| Salt, for the pasta water | 10 |
| Sourdough or ciabatta, for the side | 100 |
| Cantabrian anchovy fillets, for the toast | 25 |
| Garlic, halved, for rubbing the toast | 1 clove (5g) |

### Garlic and anchovy bowl (step 2)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Garlic cloves, thinly sliced | 15 |
| Anchovy fillets in oil (standard tin), chopped | 8 |
| Dried chilli flakes | 1 |

### Parsley and lemon bowl (step 10)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Curly parsley leaves, finely chopped | 4 |
| Lemon zest | 1 lemon (4g) |

## Method

1. **Pasta water on** · 8 min

   Biggest pan you have, filled and lidded, over high heat. Salt it heavily (**10g**).

   Start this before anything else — it is the step that stalls everything downstream.

   Take the Cantabrian anchovies (**25g**) out of the fridge now so the fat softens.

2. **Infuse the oil** · 2 min

   Into a cold wide pan: olive oil (**55g**), then tip in the garlic and anchovy bowl. Bring up to medium-low and press the anchovies with a wooden spoon until they dissolve completely.

   Cold start means the garlic infuses rather than fries — pull the pan off the heat if it colours past pale gold.

3. **Darken the purée** · 1 min

   Add the tomato purée (**15g**) and stir constantly for 30–45 seconds until it shifts from bright red to brick. This is what replaces the prawn-shell stock — do not skip it with peeled prawns.

4. **Reduce the wine** · 3 min

   Pour in the white wine (**100g**), raise the heat and reduce by half. It should stop smelling sharp.

5. **Collapse the tomatoes** · 8 min

   Add the cherry tomatoes (**250g**) cut side down and squash a few against the pan to start them releasing. Simmer until slumped and saucy, with the skins loosening.

   Taste and salt only now — the anchovies have already brought roughly 1g of salt in.

6. **Toast the bread** · 4 min

   While the tomatoes simmer: slice the bread (**100g**) at 1.5cm and char it in a dry pan or under the grill, 1–2 min a side.

   Rub the hot cut face firmly with 1 halved raw garlic clove (**5g**), drizzle with a little of the extra virgin oil and drape the Cantabrian anchovies (**25g**) on whole — 2 per slice, never chopped.

7. **Cook the pasta** · 6 min

   Spaghetti (**200g**) into the boiling water, cooked to 2 minutes under the packet time. **Fill a mug with pasta water before draining** — about 250g. Drain, do not rinse.

8. **Prawns in** · 2 min

   Raise the heat under the sauce and add the dried prawns (**300g**). Cook just until opaque, then kill the heat for the last 30 seconds.

   Doneness cue: a loose C shape. A tight O means overcooked.

9. **Toss** · 2 min

   Add the drained pasta and about **60g** of the reserved pasta water. Toss hard and continuously over the heat so the starch emulsifies the sauce and it clings to the strands.

   Add more water only if it looks tight — too much at once and it turns to soup you cannot reverse.

10. **Finish** · 1 min

    Off the heat, toss through the parsley and lemon bowl and the remaining extra virgin olive oil (**15g**). Plate immediately with the anchovy toast alongside.

## Tips

- No cheese. Parmesan flattens seafood; Sicilians use toasted breadcrumbs or, here, anchovy toast on the side.
- Save the good Cantabrian anchovies for the toast and use a cheap tin for the base — dissolving a £9 fillet into hot oil destroys exactly what you pay for.
- Zest goes in off the heat only. The oils are volatile and cook off in seconds, leaving bitterness and no aroma.
- No lemon juice in the pan — it breaks the emulsion and turns the dish sour rather than bright. Wedges on the side instead.
- Prep the night before (prawns thawed, tomatoes halved, garlic sliced) and this is a 15-minute weeknight dish.
- Olive oil, not rapeseed. The oil is a flavour here and it is what emulsifies with the pasta water.
- Scaling to 4: 5 garlic cloves not 6, 180g wine, 6 tbsp oil. Needs a 28–30cm pan or the prawns steam.
- Pat the prawns bone dry and leave them uncovered until they go in. Wet prawns steam instead of searing and go rubbery.
