# Baked Camembert with Garlic Honey and Walnuts

**Makes** 4 servings (sharing starter) · **Total** 45 min · **Active** 20 min

Molten camembert baked in its own box, studded with garlic and finished with a separately infused garlic honey and toasted walnuts. The split approach is deliberate — studded garlic gives roasted pockets, the infusion carries garlic flavour through the whole thing.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Whole camembert, in its wooden box | 1 wheel (250g) |
| Garlic cloves, thinly sliced | 3 cloves (15g) |
| Walnut halves | 50 |
| Runny honey | 63 |
| Fresh thyme sprigs | 3 |
| Olive oil | 5 |
| Flaky sea salt | 1 |
| Black pepper, freshly cracked | 1 |
| Sourdough or baguette, sliced, to serve | 200 |

### Bowl A — honey, garlic and thyme leaves, in the small pan (step 5)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Runny honey | 63 |
| Garlic cloves, thinly sliced (first portion) | 5 |
| Fresh thyme sprigs (third sprig, leaves only) | 1 (1g) |

### Bowl B — garlic and thyme for the cheese (step 6)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Garlic cloves, thinly sliced (second portion) | 10 |
| Fresh thyme sprigs (two whole sprigs) | 2 (2g) |

## Method

1. **Temper the cheese** · 30–60 min

   Take the 1 wheel (**250g**) of camembert out of the fridge and leave it wrapped at room temperature. 30 minutes for a ripe wheel, a full hour if it's young — a fridge-cold centre stays chalky while the edges split.

2. **Preheat oven** · 10 min

   180°C fan / 200°C conventional / gas 6. For a young, firm wheel drop to 170°C fan and plan on the longer bake time in step 7.

3. **Slice the garlic, pick the thyme** · 5 min

   Thinly slice the 3 garlic cloves (**15g**) and pick the leaves from the third thyme sprig (**1g**), ready for Bowls A and B.

4. **Toast the walnuts** · 5 min

   Dry-toast the walnut halves (**50g**) in a frying pan over medium heat with no oil, shaking every 30 seconds. Done when they smell nutty and the cut faces are lightly golden — smell is the cue, not colour, as walnuts turn bitter fast. Tip onto a cold plate immediately so the pan's residual heat doesn't take them further. Chop roughly once cool.

5. **Infuse the honey** · 5 min

   Warm Bowl A (honey **63g**, sliced garlic **5g** and thyme leaves **1g**) over low heat. Don't let it boil — boiled garlic turns bitter. Take off the heat and leave to steep while the cheese bakes.

6. **Prep the cheese** · 5 min

   Unwrap the camembert fully and discard the paper — the cloth-feel inner sheet is waxed or laminated and must not go in the oven. Return the naked wheel to its wooden base, lid off, and check the seam for metal staples; if there are any, wrap in a double layer of baking paper or use a buttered ramekin instead. Score a shallow crosshatch across the top rind, about 5mm deep, without cutting through the base. Push Bowl B (sliced garlic **10g** and two thyme sprigs **2g**) into the cuts. Rub with olive oil (**5g**) and season with black pepper (**1g**). Sit the whole box on a baking tray.

7. **Bake** · 18–20 min

   18–20 minutes at 180°C fan, or 22–25 minutes at 170°C fan for a young wheel. Done when the centre wobbles freely as you nudge the box. Underbaking is recoverable, overbaking isn't — a split camembert weeps oil and never comes back.

8. **Rest and finish** · 3 min

   Rest for 3 minutes, then open up the scored top. Spoon over the garlic honey (**63g**), scatter with the chopped walnuts (**50g**) and flaky sea salt (**1g**). Serve straight away with the bread (**200g**) for dipping.

## Tips

- Infuse the honey separately rather than relying only on studded garlic — studding alone leaves flavour in pockets, the infusion spreads it.
- Slice the garlic paper-thin. Chunky slices or whole cloves stay raw and harsh in both the studs and the 5-minute infusion.
- Score no deeper than 5mm and never through the base rind, or the cheese escapes into the box.
- Always bake on a tray. Burnt camembert on the oven floor is a miserable clean-up.
- Judging ripeness: press the top. Springy like the pad of your palm means young and firm. Yields easily like a relaxed cheek means ready. A young wheel improves with 7–14 more days in the fridge, or 2–3 days at cool room temperature to speed it up.
- Le Rustique boxes are glued thin wood, not stapled, so the box is oven-safe as-is — still worth a glance at the seam.
- A tablespoon of white wine or cream poured into the scored top before baking helps loosen a firm paste.
- Cornichons or a sharp chutney on the side cut the richness.
- Leftovers don't reheat. Eat it hot.
