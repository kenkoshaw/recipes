# Scones with Jam and Clotted Cream

Quick storecupboard scones from Good Food magazine, two ways. **Classic** uses warm milk with a squeeze of lemon. **Buttermilk** swaps both for cold buttermilk and uses bicarbonate of soda instead of baking powder, for a tangier, more tender scone. A hot baking tray gives a tall rise, and the Kenwood K-beater rubs in the butter.

## Ingredients (Classic)

**Makes** 8 scones · **Total** 15 min · **Active** ~10 min

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Butter, cubed | 85 |
| Caster sugar | 3 tbsp (~38g) |
| Milk | 175ml (180g) |
| Egg, beaten, to glaze | ~25 |
| Jam, to serve | to taste |
| Clotted cream, to serve | to taste |

### Mixer bowl (step 2)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Self-raising flour, plus more for dusting | 350 |
| Baking powder | 1 tsp (4g) |
| Salt | ¼ tsp (~1.5g) |

### Flavouring bowl (step 4)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Vanilla extract | 1 tsp (5g) |
| Lemon juice | 1 squeeze (~5g) |

## Method (Classic)

1. **Heat the oven and tray** · 10 min

   Heat the oven to 220°C (200°C fan). Put a baking tray in to heat up.

2. **Mix the dry ingredients** · 1 min

   Fit the K-beater and mix the mixer bowl on low until combined.

3. **Rub in the butter** · 3 min

   Add the butter (**85g**) and mix on low until the mixture looks like fine crumbs, checking it often. Stop as soon as it does. Stir in 3 tbsp (**~38g**) caster sugar.

4. **Warm the milk** · 2 min

   Microwave the 175ml (**180g**) milk in the jug for about 30 secs, until warm but not hot. Stir in the flavouring bowl and set aside for a moment.

5. **Mix the dough** · 2 min

   Take the bowl off the Kenwood. Make a well in the dry mix, pour in the milk mixture and combine it quickly with a cutlery knife. It will seem pretty wet at first.

6. **Fold and cut** · 10 min

   Scatter some flour onto the work surface and tip the dough out. Dredge the dough and your hands with a little more flour and fold the dough over 2–3 times until it's a little smoother.

   Pat it into a round about 4cm deep. Dip a 5cm cutter in flour and plunge it into the dough, repeating until you have four scones.

   Press what's left of the dough back into a round and cut out another four.

7. **Glaze and bake** · 10 min

   Brush the tops with the beaten egg (**~25g**) and carefully arrange the scones on the hot baking tray. Bake for 10 min, until risen and golden on top.

8. **Serve** · 5 min

   Eat just warm, or cold on the day of baking, generously topped with jam and clotted cream.

## Tips (Classic)

- **Lemon juice**: it acts as a buttermilk substitute and boosts the raising agents. Use buttermilk if you can get it (see the Buttermilk version), or apple cider vinegar in a pinch (from the source).
- **Warm milk**: a tip from the source for light scones that rise every time.

## Ingredients (Buttermilk)

**Makes** 8 scones · **Total** ~20 min · **Active** ~10 min

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Butter, cubed | 85 |
| Caster sugar | 3 tbsp (~38g) |
| Buttermilk, cold (up to a 284ml carton) | 200ml (~206g) |
| Vanilla extract | 1 tsp (5g) |
| Egg, beaten, to glaze | ~25 |
| Jam, to serve | to taste |
| Clotted cream, to serve | to taste |

### Mixer bowl (step 2)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Self-raising flour, plus more for dusting | 350 |
| Bicarbonate of soda | ½ tsp (~2.5g) |
| Salt | ¼ tsp (~1.5g) |

## Method (Buttermilk)

1. **Heat the oven and tray** · 10 min

   Heat the oven to 220°C (200°C fan). Put a baking tray in to heat up.

2. **Mix the dry ingredients** · 1 min

   Fit the K-beater and mix the mixer bowl on low until combined.

3. **Rub in the butter** · 3 min

   Add the butter (**85g**) and mix on low until the mixture looks like fine crumbs, checking it often. Stop as soon as it does. Stir in 3 tbsp (**~38g**) caster sugar.

4. **Flavour the buttermilk** · 1 min

   Stir 1 tsp (**5g**) vanilla into 200ml (**~206g**) cold buttermilk. Don't warm it: buttermilk splits when heated.

5. **Mix the dough** · 2 min

   Take the bowl off the Kenwood. Make a well in the dry mix, pour in the buttermilk and combine it quickly with a cutlery knife into a soft, slightly sticky dough.

   If there are dry floury bits left, add a little more buttermilk, a splash at a time.

6. **Fold and cut** · 10 min

   Work quickly from here: the bicarbonate of soda starts reacting as soon as the buttermilk goes in. Scatter some flour onto the work surface and tip the dough out. Dredge the dough and your hands with a little more flour and fold the dough over 2–3 times until it's a little smoother.

   Pat it into a round about 4cm deep. Dip a 5cm cutter in flour and plunge it into the dough, repeating until you have four scones.

   Press what's left of the dough back into a round and cut out another four.

7. **Glaze and bake** · 10–12 min

   Brush the tops with the beaten egg (**~25g**) and carefully arrange the scones on the hot baking tray. Bake for 10–12 min, until risen and golden on top.

8. **Serve** · 5 min

   Eat just warm, or cold on the day of baking, generously topped with jam and clotted cream.

## Tips (Buttermilk)

- **Why bicarbonate of soda**: buttermilk is acidic, and bicarb needs an acid to make the gas that lifts the scones, so it replaces the baking powder. Self-raising flour still does most of the rising.
- **Liquid**: buttermilk doughs run softer. BBC Good Food's buttermilk scones use a whole 284ml carton for the same 350g flour; start with 200ml and add more only if the dough is dry.
- **No buttermilk**: stir 1 tbsp (**15g**) lemon juice or white vinegar into 200ml (**206g**) milk and leave for 5–10 min until slightly thickened and curdled. Or loosen natural yoghurt with milk, about 2 parts yoghurt to 1 part milk, to make 200ml.

## Tips

- **Kenwood use**: the K-beater on low mixes the dry ingredients and rubs in the butter (steps 2–3). The wet mix and the folding stay by hand, since the source says scones are like soda bread and turn tough when over-handled.
- **Tall scones**: pat the dough out a bit thicker than you think you should. A hot baking tray kick-starts the rise, and don't leave the dough sitting around (from the source).
- **Cutter**: smooth-edged cutters cut more cleanly, giving a better rise (from the source).
- **Sultanas**: for fruit scones, stir **85g** of plump sultanas in with the sugar (from the source).
- **Freezing**: freeze once cool. Defrost, then refresh in a low oven (160°C, or 140°C fan) for a few minutes (from the source).
