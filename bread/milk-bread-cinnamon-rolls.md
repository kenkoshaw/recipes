# Milk Bread Cinnamon Rolls

**Makes** 12 rolls · **Total** ~3 hr 45 · **Active** ~1 hr

Tangzhong milk-bread dough with a brown sugar cinnamon swirl and cream cheese frosting. Kenwood dough hook for the dough, K-beater for the frosting. Can rise overnight in the fridge (see Tips).

## Method

1. **Make the tangzhong** · 5 min

   Line the 26×26cm square cake tin with baking paper.

   In a small saucepan, whisk the water (**155g**) and ¼ cup (**32g**) bread flour. Cook over medium heat, whisking constantly, for 4–5 min until it turns into a thick paste.

   Scrape it into a small bowl and leave to cool until only slightly warm. If it's hot, it will kill the yeast.

2. **Mix the dough** · 2 min

   Fit the dough hook to the mixer bowl and mix on low for a few seconds to combine.

   Add the wet jug and the cooled tangzhong. Knead on low for 2 min until it forms a rough, shaggy dough ball.

3. **Knead in the butter, then rest** · 15 min + 30 min rest

   On low speed, add the 8 tbsp (**113g**) unsalted butter one piece at a time, letting each fully disappear before adding the next.

   Keep kneading on low for 10–12 min, until the dough pulls away from the sides and feels smooth, stretchy and elastic.

   Take the dough out and pull the sides down underneath into a smooth ball. Put it seam side down back in the bowl, cover with cling film and rest for 30 min. It isn't meant to rise much: the rest relaxes the gluten so it rolls out easily.

4. **Mix the filling** · 5 min

   While the dough rests, stir the filling bowl into a smooth paste, so the salt is spread evenly. The butter must be cool room temperature: if it's melted or greasy, the filling leaks out in the oven.

5. **Roll, fill and cut** · 20 min

   On a lightly floured surface, roll the dough into a 38×53cm rectangle. It should need little to no flour.

   Dollop the filling over the dough and spread it to the edges with an offset spatula, leaving a clean 2.5cm border along one long edge.

   With a pizza wheel, cut 12 strips about 4.5cm wide, cutting across the long side so each strip runs from the clean border to the opposite edge.

   Roll each strip up towards the clean border and set it in the tin, cut side up, in 3 rows of 4.

6. **Final rise** · 1–2 hr

   Cover the tin with cling film and leave in a warm place until doubled in size.

   When they're nearly there, heat the oven to 160°C (140°C fan).

7. **Bake** · 30–35 min

   Bake for 30–35 min, until the tops are lightly golden and the centre roll reads 85°C on a probe thermometer. The rolls sit a little taller in the square tin, so they may need up to 5 min more: go by the thermometer.

8. **Make the frosting** · 10 min

   While the rolls bake, wash and dry the Kenwood bowl and fit the K-beater.

   Beat the cold cream cheese (**142g**) and 6 tbsp (**84g**) unsalted butter on low until combined. Add the icing sugar (**120g**) and keep beating on low until light and fluffy.

   Mix in ½ tsp (**~3g**) vanilla bean paste and 1 tbsp (**15g**) double cream.

9. **Frost and serve** · 30 min

   Cool the tin on a wire rack for 15 min, then spread the frosting over the warm rolls.

   Leave for another 15 min so the frosting melts into the centres. Serve warm.

## Tips

- **Overnight rise**: after step 5, cover the tin and chill overnight, no longer than 24 hr or the dough turns sour. In the morning, leave it somewhere warm until doubled (1–3 hr, it depends on the kitchen), then bake.
- **Freezing**: after step 5, wrap the tin in 2 layers of cling film and foil and freeze for up to 3 weeks. Defrost overnight in the fridge, then rise until doubled and bake.
- **Metal tin**: the source recommends metal. In glass or ceramic, bake at 175°C (155°C fan) instead.
- **Vanilla extract** works in place of vanilla bean paste, in the same amount (from the source).
- **Kenwood use**: the dough hook kneads the dough (steps 2–3) and the K-beater makes the frosting (step 8). The source says it can be done by hand too.
- **Storing**: unfrosted rolls keep 4–5 days in an airtight box at room temperature; frosted ones go in the fridge. A damp piece of kitchen roll in the box keeps them soft. Warm in the oven or microwave before serving.
