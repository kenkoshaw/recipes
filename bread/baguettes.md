# Baguettes

No-knead baguettes folded in a lidded tub. Sugar replaces malt powder for colour and a slight sweetness. Ice on the tray gives the steam that opens the scores. **Overnight** proves 12 hr in the fridge for more flavour and a firmer dough that's easier to shape and score. **Same-day** proves at room temperature with more yeast, and is ready in about 4 hr.

## Shopping List (Overnight)

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Strong white bread flour | 150g |
| Plain flour | 150g |
| Caster sugar | 50g |
| Fast action dried yeast | 1 sachet |

## Ingredients (Overnight)

**Makes** 2 baguettes · **Total** ~16 hr (12 hr in the fridge) · **Active** 30 min

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Lukewarm water (body temperature) | 180 |
| Strong white bread flour (for dusting) | ~15 |
| Ice cubes | 4–5 |

### Dry mix bowl (step 1)

Weigh straight into a lidded tub of about 2 litres; the dough stays in it until morning.

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Strong white bread flour | 125 |
| Plain flour | 125 |
| Caster sugar | 10 |
| Salt | 5 |
| Fast action dried yeast | 2 |

## Method (Overnight)

1. **Mix the dough** · 5 min

   Stir the dry mix bowl to combine. Add the lukewarm water (**180g**) and mix with a spatula until no dry flour is left. Scrape the dough into the centre of the tub.

   Put the lid on and leave at room temperature for 30 min.

2. **First fold** · 30 min

   Fold the dough 25 times: lift an edge, fold it into the centre, turn the tub a little and repeat.

   Put the lid on and leave at room temperature for 30 min.

3. **Second fold** · 30 min

   Fold 25 times as before. Flip the dough over so the smooth side is on top.

   Put the lid on and leave at room temperature for 30 min.

4. **Third fold, then chill** · 12 hr

   Fold 25 times as before and flip the smooth side up again.

   Put the lid on and leave in the fridge for 10–15 hr. The original was made with 12 hr.

5. **Warm up and turn out** · 1 hr

   Take the tub out of the fridge and leave at room temperature for 1 hr.

   Dust the top of the dough with dusting flour (**~5g**) so it won't stick. Gently ease the dough away from the sides of the tub. Turn the tub over onto a floured surface and let the dough fall out on its own.

6. **Divide and rest** · 20 min

   Weigh the dough (about **445g**) and cut it into 2 pieces of about **220g**. Cover and rest for 20 min.

7. **Shape** · 5 min

   Pat each piece into a rectangle with the pads of your fingers. Tap gently to spread the bubbles evenly; don't press hard with your fingertips.

   Fold the top third down and the bottom third up. Fold in half lengthways, seal the seam and roll to a baguette as long as your baking tray allows.

8. **Final prove and heat the oven** · 35 min

   Put the baguettes seam side down on a baking tray lined with baking paper. Leave at room temperature for 35 min.

   Heat the oven to 250°C as soon as the prove starts, so it is fully hot when the prove ends.

9. **Score** · 2 min

   Dust the baguettes with dusting flour (**~5g**). Score each one with a razor blade held almost flat to the dough. Put the ice cubes on the tray, beside the baguettes.

10. **Bake** · 20 min

    Turn the oven down to 230°C. Spray the inside of the oven about 25 times with a water spray bottle. Put the tray in, spray about 10 more times and close the door.

    Bake for 20 min or more, until deep golden. Ovens colour differently, so add time as needed.

## Method (Same-day)

1. **Mix the dough** · 5 min

   Stir the dry mix bowl to combine. Add the lukewarm water (**180g**) and mix with a spatula until no dry flour is left. Scrape the dough into the centre of the tub.

   Put the lid on and leave at room temperature for 30 min.

2. **First fold** · 30 min

   Fold the dough 25 times: lift an edge, fold it into the centre, turn the tub a little and repeat.

   Put the lid on and leave at room temperature for 30 min.

3. **Second fold** · 30 min

   Fold 25 times as before. Flip the dough over so the smooth side is on top.

   Put the lid on and leave at room temperature for 30 min.

4. **Third fold, then rise** · 1 hr

   Fold 25 times as before and flip the smooth side up again.

   Put the lid on and leave at room temperature for 1–1½ hr, until the dough has grown about 1.5 times. Go by size, not the clock: a cool kitchen takes longer.

5. **Turn out** · 2 min

   Dust the top of the dough with dusting flour (**~5g**) so it won't stick. Gently ease the dough away from the sides of the tub. Turn the tub over onto a floured surface and let the dough fall out on its own.

6. **Divide and rest** · 20 min

   Weigh the dough (about **445g**) and cut it into 2 pieces of about **220g**. Cover and rest for 20 min.

7. **Shape** · 5 min

   Pat each piece into a rectangle with the pads of your fingers. Tap gently to spread the bubbles evenly; don't press hard with your fingertips.

   The dough is softer and stickier than the overnight version, so flour your hands lightly. Fold the top third down and the bottom third up. Fold in half lengthways, seal the seam and roll to a baguette as long as your baking tray allows.

8. **Final prove and heat the oven** · 30 min

   Put the baguettes seam side down on a baking tray lined with baking paper. Leave at room temperature for 30 min, until puffy; warm dough proves faster than cold.

   Heat the oven to 250°C as soon as the prove starts, so it is fully hot when the prove ends.

9. **Score** · 2 min

   Dust the baguettes with dusting flour (**~5g**). Score each one with a razor blade held almost flat to the dough. Put the ice cubes on the tray, beside the baguettes.

10. **Bake** · 20 min

    Turn the oven down to 230°C. Spray the inside of the oven about 25 times with a water spray bottle. Put the tray in, spray about 10 more times and close the door.

    Bake for 20 min or more, until deep golden. Ovens colour differently, so add time as needed.

## Tips (Same-day)

- For more flavour on the same day, keep the yeast at 2g and let the dough rise for 2–3 hr in step 4.

## Tips

- A blade held almost flat, cutting under the skin, opens the scores better than a straight-down cut.
- Ice on the tray opens the scores most cleanly of the methods the author tried.
- The folds build the gluten, so this dough isn't kneaded. The Kenwood isn't needed here: the gentle folds in the tub keep the open crumb.
