# Burger Buns

Burger buns two ways. **Brioche** is the proper version: a lean brioche with a shreddy crumb, an optional overnight prove, and buns that keep to day three. **Fast** takes about 1 hr 30: the crumb is fluffier and more bread-roll than brioche and it stales by day two, but it's still very good under a burger.

## Ingredients (Brioche)

**Makes** 8 buns (110g each) · **Total** 3 hr 50 (or 10–13 hr over two days) · **Active** 40 min

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Large eggs | 2 (110g) |
| Unsalted butter, softened and cubed | 100 |
| Large egg, for the glaze | 1 (55g) |
| Sesame seeds | 2 tbsp (18g) |

### Yeast jug (step 1)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Fast-action dried yeast | 7 |
| Whole milk, lukewarm | 200ml (200g) |

### Mixer bowl (step 2)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Strong white bread flour | 500 |
| Caster sugar | 50 |
| Fine salt | 10 |

## Method (Brioche)

1. **Activate the yeast** · 10 min

   Whisk the yeast jug together with a pinch of the sugar from the mixer bowl. Leave until foamy and smelling bready — flat means dead yeast, start again.

2. **Mix the dough** · 5 min

   Fit the mixer bowl to the stand mixer with the dough hook and mix briefly to combine. Add the yeasted milk and 2 eggs (**110g**). Mix on low, then medium, until a rough cohesive dough forms.

3. **Add the butter** · 10 min

   Add the butter (**100g**) a few cubes at a time, waiting for each addition to disappear. Knead on medium until glossy, smooth and pulling cleanly from the bowl sides.

   Should pass the windowpane test — stretch a piece thin enough to see light through without tearing.

4. **First prove** · 1 hr 30, or overnight in fridge

   Cover and prove at room temperature until roughly doubled. Cold overnight prove (8–12 hrs) gives better flavour and much easier shaping.

5. **Shape** · 10 min

   Knock back, divide into 8 pieces of **~110g**. Shape into tight balls by dragging in circles under a cupped hand on an unfloured surface. Place on a lined tray 5 cm apart, press tops gently flat.

6. **Second prove** · 1 hr

   Cover loosely, prove until visibly puffy.

   Poke test with a floured finger: springs straight back = needs longer; dents and slowly fills halfway = ready; stays fully dented = overproved, knock back and reshape. Don't rush — underproved buns bake dense.

7. **Glaze and bake** · 17 min

   Oven to 180°C fan / 200°C conventional. Beat 1 glaze egg (**55g**) with a splash of milk, brush thoroughly, scatter 2 tbsp (**18g**) sesame seeds. Bake until deep golden all over, hollow-sounding underneath, internal temp 88–90°C.

8. **Cool completely** · 30 min

   Wire rack. Slicing warm brioche compresses the crumb. Really pushed: 10 min minimum, serrated knife, sawing motion, no downward pressure.

## Tips (Brioche)

- 20% butter is deliberate. True brioche runs 50%+, which collapses under sauce. This is a lean brioche: rich crumb, still structurally sound.
- Butter goes in after gluten development, never before — otherwise it coats the flour and you'll never build structure.
- Sticky dough is normal. Cold-prove overnight to fix it. Same-day, chill 20 min before shaping rather than adding flour.
- Straight sides: bake in a ring mould or with buns nearly touching, rather than spaced.
- Upgrade — add milk powder (**20g**) with the flour for a softer, longer-lasting crumb.
- Upgrade — tangzhong: cook **25g** of the flour with **125g** of the milk to a paste, cool, add at step 2. Noticeably plusher and keeps better.
- Storage: best day one. Freeze split and pre-sliced, then toast from frozen — better than day-three fresh.

## Ingredients (Fast)

**Makes** 8 buns (110g each) · **Total** ~1 hr 30 · **Active** ~20 min

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Large egg, for the glaze | 1 (55g) |
| Sesame seeds | 2 tbsp (18g) |

### Yeast jug (step 1)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Fast-action dried yeast | 14 |
| Whole milk, warm (~38°C) | 250ml (250g) |
| Caster sugar (for the yeast) | 1 pinch (~1g) |

### Mixer bowl (step 2)

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Strong white bread flour | 500 |
| Caster sugar | 39 |
| Fine salt | 9 |
| Large egg | 1 (55g) |
| Unsalted butter, melted and cooled slightly | 70 |

## Method (Fast)

1. **Wake the yeast** · 5 min

   Whisk the yeast jug together. Leave until lively and foamy.

2. **Mix and knead** · 8 min

   Fit the mixer bowl to the mixer. Add the yeast jug.

   Knead on medium until smooth and elastic — 8 min in a mixer, 10 by hand. Tacky but shouldn't stick to a clean finger.

3. **Divide and shape** · 5 min

   Skip the first prove entirely. Divide into 8 pieces of **~110g**, roll into tight balls, space 5 cm apart on a lined tray. Press tops flat.

4. **Single prove** · 40 min

   Cover loosely, prove somewhere warm until nearly doubled and pillowy — poke test: dents and slowly fills halfway = ready. Heat oven to 190°C fan / 210°C conventional partway through.

5. **Glaze and bake** · 15 min

   Beat 1 glaze egg (**55g**) with a splash of milk, brush generously, scatter 2 tbsp (**18g**) sesame seeds. Bake until deep golden — higher heat compensates for the shorter prove. Internal temp 88–90°C.

6. **Cool** · 20 min

   Twenty minutes is the realistic minimum before the crumb sets enough to slice cleanly.

## Tips (Fast)

- Time savings come from: double yeast (**14g**) + warm prove (fine here — short prove, no time for boozy off-flavours); melted butter added with the wet ingredients rather than worked in after gluten development; one prove instead of two (costs a little height and evenness).
- Warm spot for proving: oven with just the light on, or the tray sat over a roasting tin of hot water. Don't exceed ~30°C or the butter weeps out.

## Tips

- Sizing: 110g = standard burger. 130g for smash-stacks, 80g for sliders.
