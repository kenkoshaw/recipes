# Melon Pan

**Makes** 10 buns · **Total** 3 hr 15 · **Active** ~1 hr 15

Classic Japanese sweet bread: a soft enriched bun under a thin, crisp cookie (biscuit) crust with the signature gridline pattern. The bread dough is kneaded with the Kenwood dough hook and the biscuit dough is mixed with the K-beater.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Grams |
|------------|-------|
| Bread flour, plus extra for dusting | 225 |
| Cake flour (bread dough) | 25 |
| Salt (Diamond Crystal kosher) | 3 |
| Granulated sugar (bread dough) | 40 |
| Instant yeast | 4 |
| Large egg, beaten (bread dough) | 50 |
| Whole milk, at 30°C | 50 |
| Water, at 30°C | 50 |
| Unsalted butter, cubed, room temperature (bread dough) | 35 |
| Unsalted butter, cubed, room temperature (biscuit dough) | 60 |
| Granulated sugar (biscuit dough) | 100 |
| Large egg, beaten (biscuit dough) | 50 |
| Cake flour (biscuit dough) | 200 |
| Baking powder | 2 |
| Granulated sugar, for the topping | 26 |

## Method

1. **Prep everything** · 20 min

   Line two baking trays with parchment or silicone mats. Weigh the bread flour (**225g**), cake flour (**25g**), salt (**3g**), sugar (**40g**) and yeast (**4g**) straight into the Kenwood bowl, since they go in together in step 2. Warm the milk (**50g**) and water (**50g**) to 30°C, then beat in 1 large egg (**50g**) in the same jug, since all of it goes in at once in step 2. Cube the butter (**35g**) into a small bowl for step 3. Weigh the butter (**60g**) and sugar (**100g**) for the biscuit dough into one bowl for step 5, and the cake flour (**200g**) and baking powder (**2g**) into another to be sifted together in step 5. Leave the second egg (**50g**) in its shell until step 5. Weigh the topping sugar (**26g**) into a small bowl and keep a little bread flour to hand for dusting.

2. **Mix the bread dough** · 5 min

   Whisk the dry ingredients in the Kenwood bowl by hand to combine. Fit the dough hook, pour in the jug of egg (**50g**), milk (**50g**) and water (**50g**), and start on low speed. Scrape the sides and bottom with a silicone spatula a couple of times. Keep mixing until the dough clings to the hook and cleans the sides of the bowl. It starts very sticky.

3. **Knead in the butter** · ~10 min

   Once the dough starts to come together, add the butter cubes (**35g**) with the mixer on low and mix until no streaks remain. Raise to medium-low and knead until the dough is silky, smooth and elastic. Do the windowpane test: gently pull the dough into a square. If it stretches to a thin membrane that light passes through without tearing, it's ready. If it tears, knead for 2 more minutes and test again.

4. **First rise** · 1–2 hr

   Form the dough into a ball by pulling all the edges to the bottom and pinching them together. Put it seam side down in a separate bowl, since the Kenwood bowl is needed in step 5. Cover with plastic wrap and leave in a warm place until doubled in size. Use your oven's proof setting (38°C) if it has one. If not, put small dishes of boiling water at the four corners of the lower-middle rack, put the dough bowl in the centre and close the door. Make the biscuit dough while it rises.

5. **Make the biscuit dough** · 15 min

   Wash and dry the Kenwood bowl and fit the K-beater. Add the butter (**60g**) and sugar (**100g**) and mix on medium-low until smooth and well blended. Crack the second egg (**50g**), beat it and add it a very small amount at a time, blending well after each addition, until it's all in. Sift in the cake flour (**200g**) and baking powder (**2g**) and mix until just incorporated, then stop the mixer. Take the dough out and form it into a ball by hand.

6. **Divide and chill the biscuit dough** · 15 min

   Weigh the biscuit dough (it should be around **400–410g**) and cut it roughly into 10 pieces. Divide the total by 10 for the target weight of each piece (**40–41g**). Weigh the biggest piece first and pinch off dough if it's over, then weigh again. Repeat with the others, pinching dough from larger pieces or pushing some into smaller ones. Roll each piece into a ball, put them on a lined tray, cover with plastic wrap and chill for 10 min so they firm up and are easier to handle.

7. **Check and divide the bread dough** · 15 min

   Dust a finger with flour and poke the centre of the risen dough. If the hole stays, it's ready. If it closes straight away, let it rise a little longer. Tip the dough onto a lightly floured surface and press it to deflate. Fold the sides in thirds to the centre, then the bottom and top in thirds, flip it seam side down and roll it into a ball, turning it so the seam closes. Weigh it (it should be around **460–470g**) and cut it into 10 pieces. The target for each piece is the total divided by 10 (**46–47g**). Weigh and adjust the pieces as in step 6. Pre-shape each into a ball by pulling the edges underneath and turning it on your palm with the seam at the bottom. Put them on the second lined tray, cover with plastic wrap and rest for 15 min at room temperature.

8. **Roll the biscuit dough** · 10 min

   While the bread dough rests, flatten each ball of biscuit dough on a lightly floured surface. Roll each one with a rolling pin into a flat circle about 10–12cm across and set aside.

9. **Shape the bread dough** · 5 min

   Flatten each rested ball of bread dough with your hand. Fold the top and bottom in thirds to the centre, then the left and right sides, and pinch the ends together into a ball. Pull the dough from all sides towards the seam, then turn it on your palm a few times with the seam at the bottom. Set aside and repeat with the rest.

10. **Wrap and score** · 15 min

    Put a biscuit circle on your palm and a bread ball in the middle, seam side up. Flip them over together so the biscuit dough is on top and gently pull the biscuit dough down around the bread ball, leaving the base uncovered. Dip the biscuit dough in the topping sugar (**26g**) and shake off the excess. Score a crisscross pattern about 3mm deep with a dough scraper or knife, 3 lines in each direction. Put each on a lined tray, biscuit side up, and cover with plastic wrap.

11. **Final rise** · 50 min

    Leave the dough in a warm place until it has grown to about 1½ times its size. When it's about halfway there, heat the oven to 180°C with a rack in the middle. If you're using the oven's proof setting, move the dough to another warm place first so the oven can heat.

12. **Bake** · 13–15 min

    Remove the plastic wrap and bake at 180°C for 13–15 min, until the biscuit crust is light golden. If it's browning unevenly towards the end, turn the tray once. Don't overbake or the buns will be dry. Move them to a wire rack and cool for 5 min before eating.

## Tips

- **Kenwood use**: the dough hook kneads the bread dough (steps 2–3) and the K-beater mixes the biscuit dough (step 5). Shaping, dividing and rolling stay by hand. The source's mixer speeds (2, then 4 or 6) are for its own mixer, not a Kenwood, so these steps say "low" and "medium-low" instead. Check your Kenwood manual for the top speed it allows with the dough hook.
- **One bowl**: the bread dough rises in a separate bowl so the Kenwood bowl is free for the biscuit dough.
- **Whole milk**: use whole milk, not reduced-fat (from the source).
- **Kneading by hand**: the source's alternative is about 5 min of kneading, kneading in the butter, then about 10 min of slam-and-fold until it passes the windowpane test.
- **Active dry yeast**: dissolve it in the milk warmed to 43°C before adding it to the rest (from the source), instead of the 30°C used above.
- **Storing**: once completely cool, wrap each bun in plastic, then keep in an airtight container or bag in the fridge for up to 3 days, or freeze for up to a month. Defrost overnight in the fridge or in the microwave to warm the inside, then bake at 180°C until crisp on the outside.
